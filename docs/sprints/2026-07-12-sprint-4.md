### Item 2: Validate The Riskiest Go-To-Market Assumption (Research Spike)

- [x] **Status:** Done
- **Module/area:** `:votes`, `docs`
- **User value:** P0's feature line is shaped by evidence about what actually makes server owners switch off VotingPlugin, instead of by the untested assumption that "better setup UX" alone wins installs.
- **Release target:** `Not release-relevant`
- **Changelog impact:** `None`
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - [x] A written research brief covering: VotingPlugin/NuVotifier pain points, concrete switching triggers, table-stakes vs power-user features, the competitive landscape (VotingPlugin, SuperbVote, alternatives — sentiment/maintenance/licensing), an evidence-based verdict on the "setup UX is enough" assumption, and a 3–5 item recommendation for P0.
  - [x] Findings are cited to public sources; low-confidence claims are flagged; no fabricated download numbers.
  - [x] Brief saved to `docs/research/2026-07-12-votingplugin-switching-research.md` and its recommendation reflected in the `:votes` P0 backlog item.
- **Validation:**
  - [x] Brief reviewed; P0 backlog item rewritten to match the recommendation (segment choice, single-jar + testable-first-vote, service-name footgun guard, one retention hook, distribution as product work, FirstSpawn badge de-emphasized for acquisition).
- **Key finding:** "Better setup UX" is a **weak–moderate** wedge for *stealing* installed VotingPlugin, but a **strong** wedge for winning greenfield servers and replacing the abandoned NuVotifier stack. The real differentiator is **single-jar + a testable 5-minute first vote**, not prettier YAML. Distribution is product work (VoteRewards markets our thesis, has ~37 downloads).
